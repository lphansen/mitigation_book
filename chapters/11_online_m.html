M. Sensitivity analysis details: IES

M. Sensitivity analysis details: IES#

From Appendix B of the paper:

We adopt a recursive representation of preferences in continuous time for the planner extended to allow \(\rho \ne 1.\) We start by forming the continuation value for each calendar date as follows:

\[ \exp(V_t) = \left( \delta \int_0^\infty \exp( - \delta \tau) \left(C_{t+\tau}/N_{t+\tau} \right)^{1-\rho} d \tau \right)^{\frac 1 {1-\rho}} \]

where \(\exp(V)\) gives an ordinally equivalent representation of preferences since \(\exp(\cdot)\) is an increasing function.

From Appendix F of the paper:

To reduce the extensive computational cost of calculating the sensitivity analysis outcomes, we use a coarse grid resolution rather than the fine grid resolution applied for our main analysis. As shown in Appendix G, this grid choice has no impact on the qualitative implications of the model solutions, and only minor quantitative impacts that are most noticeable in the more risk aversion case.

Table 19 shows the results for two alternative specifications of the IES that differ from unity: higher (\(\rho = 3/4\)) and lower (\(\rho = 4/3\)) IES scenarios. As expected from growth models, investments in both types of capital relative to output are higher when the elasticity is greater (\(\rho\) is smaller). Conversely, emissions decrease when the elasticity is greater (\(\rho\) is smaller).
